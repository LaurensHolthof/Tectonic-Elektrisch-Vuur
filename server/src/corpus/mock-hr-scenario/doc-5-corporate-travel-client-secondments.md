Section 1: Scope of Corporate Business Travel and Client Deployments
This directive establishes the mandatory financial authorization thresholds, expense allowances, and compliance governance for mandatory business travel, technical client on-site deployments, and prolonged international expatriate secondments instructed by enterprise management. This policy strictly governs enterprise-sponsored travel conducted for the explicit commercial benefit of the enterprise and does not apply to non-business personal travel, casual holiday arrangements, or domestic commuting.

Section 2: Daily Per Diem Allowances and Corporate Credit Card Expense Caps
Personnel traveling internationally on approved commercial client secondments are entitled to a standard meal and incidentals per diem capped strictly at seventy-five euros (€75.00) per calendar day in Tier 1 European cities, or sixty-five dollars ($65.00) in other global regions. All travel expenses, client entertainment, regional ground transportation, and accommodation must be booked via our designated corporate travel management portal (American Express Global Business Travel / Concur) using the employee’s issued corporate MasterCard. Hotel nightly rates exceeding one hundred and eighty euros (€180.00) require advance written dispensation from the Corporate Financial Controller. Personal leisure days or side-trips booked alongside business travel must be billed to personal cards; mixing personal expenses on corporate credit cards constitutes a severe compliance breach resulting in immediate expense clawback.

Section 3: Expatriate Tax Equalization and Relocation Gross-Up
Employees placed on long-term client secondments or international expatriate assignments exceeding one hundred and eighty (180) consecutive days are subject to mandatory international tax equalization protocols overseen by Ernst & Young (EY). The expatriate employee must surrender their local fiscal tax domicile, complete pre-departure statutory withholding tax exit interviews, and submit to shadow payroll processing in the host country to prevent double taxation. The company provides a tax gross-up on housing allowances and temporary relocation stipends for assignments lasting over six months.

Section 4: Export Control of IT Assets and Hardware Cryptography
Employees transporting company laptops, test equipment, or encrypted development servers across international borders for client on-site demonstrations must register all serial numbers with the Corporate IT Security and Customs Export Compliance desk at least thirty (30) days prior to transit. Under international dual-use export control laws (EAR / EU Dual-Use Regulation), high-performance cryptographic laptops may not be imported into embargoed or high-risk jurisdictions without an approved statutory export license. Failure to file an international asset transit declaration may result in asset confiscation at customs border control and personal liability.

Section 5: Unscheduled Travel Modifications and Strict Prohibition of Unauthorized Telework
Employees traveling on business secondments are strictly prohibited from changing flight itineraries, extending hotel reservations, or attempting to perform unauthorized telecommuting or remote work from alternate foreign destinations without formal written authorization from the Chief Financial Officer and General Counsel. Engaging in unauthorized telework during commercial travel voids corporate travel insurance coverage and subjects the employee to immediate cancellation of travel stipends and formal disciplinary dismissal.
